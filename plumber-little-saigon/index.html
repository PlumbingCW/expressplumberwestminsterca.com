.

Little Saigon is a business district more than a housing tract. It runs along Bolsa Avenue in Westminster, and it is known for Vietnamese restaurants, markets, bakeries, salons and strip centers. That mix puts steady demand on drains, hot water and shutoff valves.

This page is for owners and managers of those businesses, and for anyone who lives nearby and wants to know who handles what. It separates what sources confirm from general plumbing advice and from what nobody can say for certain.

Where Little Saigon sits in Westminster

.

In February 1988 the Westminster Redevelopment Agency approved a resolution naming about 1.5 miles of Bolsa Avenue the Little Saigon Tourist Commercial District. The name has stuck, and freeway signs now point to it.

Sources draw the edges differently. One describes the district as the blocks around Bolsa Avenue, Magnolia Street, Westminster Boulevard and Euclid Street, and the wider Little Saigon community also reaches into Garden Grove, Fountain Valley and Santa Ana. If your address is outside the city of Westminster, a different city and sewer district may apply.

The Asian Garden Mall at 9200 Bolsa Avenue and the Bolsa Avenue and Moran Street intersection are two well known anchors. Both help a driver or a caller describe a location, but neither marks an official boundary.

Grease rules every kitchen on Bolsa Avenue should know

.

Midway City Sanitary District Ordinance 63 prohibits discharging wastewater with fat, oil or grease into the district sewer. Food service businesses are expected to use a grease interceptor or trap to catch it first.

The district says the business is responsible for maintaining the device. Its program describes a typical interceptor as 1,500 to 2,000 gallons with two or three chambers, usually buried outside. It calls for monthly inspection, and for a full cleaning by a licensed grease service at least twice a year.

The district also warns against emulsifiers, biological treatments and caustic cleaners. It says these do not make grease harmless and can move the problem further down the sewer. A plumber can look at the pipes around a trap, but pumping the device is a separate trade.

Drains in salons, bakeries and shops

.

Salons see hair and product buildup in shampoo bowls and floor drains. Bakeries and markets see flour paste, starch and wipes. Both lead to slow drains that return soon after a quick clear.

When a clog comes back within weeks, the cause may sit farther down the line than the sink. A camera can show a belly, a joint or a cleanout problem, and the sewer camera inspection page explains the process. For an active clog, drain cleaning is the usual first step, and hydro jetting is used when buildup is heavy.

Hard water and hot water demand

.

The City of Westminster reports average hardness of 252 mg/L, with a range from 138 to 413 across the system. Water this hard leaves mineral scale inside heaters, dishwashers and beverage equipment over time.

That is general wear, not a claim about any one building. Regular flushing of heaters helps, and the water heater repair page covers the signs of a heater that is struggling. Businesses that run hot water all day should plan for it.

Leaks, shutoffs and the landlord question

.

In a shared plaza, the first problem in a leak is often reaching the right valve. A suite valve may be behind a locked door or in a space the landlord controls. Learn where yours is before a plumbing emergency starts, and find out who has the key.

A water bill that climbs or a meter that keeps moving after closing time can point to a hidden leak, and leak detection works through it without opening walls first. For a business problem that does not fit these pages, see commercial plumbing.

Who to ask.

The City Building Division at (714) 548-3254 can say whether a permit applies to your work. The Water Division at (714) 894-3796 handles main breaks. The sanitary district at (714) 893-3553 handles sewer overflows in the street.

Standards and local sources.

For a business address, work on pipes and grease devices is governed by the California Plumbing Code (Title 24, Part 5) and by the sewer district's own rules. Permit questions go to the City Building Division. Check any plumber's license on the Contractors State License Board website.

Little Saigon plumbing questions

.
Is Little Saigon a residential neighborhood?

It is mainly a commercial and cultural district along Bolsa Avenue. Homes sit nearby, but sources describe the district itself by its businesses, so this page leans toward business plumbing.

Are all Little Saigon addresses inside Westminster?

No. The wider Little Saigon area also reaches into Garden Grove, Fountain Valley and Santa Ana. Your city decides which permits and which utilities apply, so confirm the city on your bill first.

Who handles sewer problems on Bolsa Avenue?

The Midway City Sanitary District serves all of Westminster. Call its office at (714) 893-3553 about overflows in the street, and ask where its responsibility ends and yours begins.

Does a restaurant need a grease interceptor?

The district requires food service businesses to use an interceptor or trap and to keep it maintained. Ask the district and your landlord what applies to your suite and who maintains a shared unit.

Can a plumber pump my grease trap?

Pumping is a separate trade handled by a licensed grease service. A plumber can look at the pipes around the trap and clear drains, and a camera can show whether grease has built up in the line.
