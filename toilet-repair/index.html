.

A toilet that overflows, leaks at the floor or runs all night can be fixed fast once you know which part failed.

Most toilet problems come down to a handful of parts: the flapper, the fill valve, the wax ring and the flange. A toilet that backs up while other drains gurgle is a different problem, covered below. For a brand new fixture, see plumbing installation.

Emergency overflow: stop the water before you grab the mop

.

When a bowl is rising toward the rim, the fastest fix is the shutoff, not the plunger. Close the oval valve at the wall and lift the tank lid. Push the flapper down into its seat to stop water flowing from the tank.

If water is already on the floor, shut off the main valve at the house and move rugs and cabinet contents. Keep the lid down on a bowl that is still rising, since clean tank water is not what is overflowing. Call once the water is under control.

Toilet clog or main line backup? Check the other drains first

.

A single clogged toilet usually clears with a plunger or closet auger. Pour a bucket of water into the bowl to check if it drains slowly or not at all.

If the tub gurgles when you flush, a floor drain bubbles, or several fixtures back up together, the blockage is further down the line. That is a main line problem, and a sewer line cleaning visit is the right fix rather than more plunging. Homes across Westminster are served by the Midway City Sanitary District, which is why a recurring backup is worth looking at with a camera rather than guessing.

A toilet that runs, hisses or flushes weakly

.

A running toilet wastes water and raises your bill. The usual culprits are a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that does not shut off, or a chain that is too short or too long.

Weak flushes come from clogged rim jets or a partly blocked trap. Mineral buildup from hard water closes the small holes under the rim over time.

Leaking at the base: wax rings and flanges

.

Water seeping from the base of the toilet is not always from the bowl. A failed wax ring lets sewage-tinged water escape at every flush. The toilet may also rock, stain the floor or smell.

The fix is to pull the toilet, scrape off the old wax, check the closet flange and set the toilet on a fresh seal. A cracked or corroded flange needs a repair ring or replacement. Left alone, a slow leak can rot the subfloor.

Check the floor, not just the seal.

Soft or discolored flooring around the base means the leak has been going for a while. Tell us when you call so the subfloor can be checked while the toilet is out.

What Westminster's 1971 housing age means for your toilet

.

The median Westminster home was built around 1971, so many toilets sit on original flanges and old supply valves. Cast iron and early plastic flanges corrode or crack, especially where a toilet has been re-seated several times.

Old chrome shutoff valves also seize. If yours will not close, a plumber may replace it first so the rest of the repair can be done without turning off the whole house. You can also have angle stops replaced at the same visit.

Hard water scale inside the tank and bowl

.

Westminster water averages 252 mg/L hardness, roughly 14.7 grains per gallon. That scale coats the fill valve, the flapper seat and the rim holes. A flapper that is fine on paper can still leak because scale roughens the seat.

Cleaning the seat with vinegar and a pad sometimes restores the seal for a while. Fill valves are inexpensive parts, and swapping one is often the better fix than fighting scale.

Repair, reset or replace?

.

Repair is right when the porcelain is sound and the problem is internal parts. Replacement makes sense with a cracked bowl or tank, a chronic clog from an old design, or a model that wastes water.

Cost depends on the part and the access. A flapper or fill valve is a small job. Resetting a toilet on a new wax ring and flange repair costs more, and a new toilet costs the most. We give you a clear price before work starts.

Toilet repair questions from Westminster homeowners

.
Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Westminster?

Yes. The phone line is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe what the toilet is doing, and shut off the oval valve behind it while you wait.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A worn flapper or a fill valve that will not shut off is the usual cause. Scale from hard water can also keep the flapper from sealing. Both are quick repairs.

How do I know if my wax ring has failed?

Look for water around the base after flushing, a toilet that rocks, or a sewer smell in the bathroom. Stained or soft flooring is another sign. The toilet has to be lifted to replace the ring.

My toilet is clogged and the tub gurgles too. Is that normal?

No. When more than one fixture is affected, the blockage is likely in the main line, not the toilet. Stop using water and call for line cleaning.

Can I fix a running toilet myself?

Many people can. Try adjusting the chain, cleaning the flapper seat or replacing the flapper. If water still runs, the fill valve or seat is worn, and a plumber can fix it quickly.

Should I use chemical drain cleaner in a toilet?

No. It rarely clears the blockage, damages seals and creates a hazard for whoever works on the toilet next. A plunger or auger is safer.
