Toilet Tank Valve Repair for Westminster, CA Homes

.

Most running or hissing toilets come down to two parts inside the tank, the fill valve and the flapper, and both are manageable repairs.

Written by Westminster Express Plumbing. Published October 24, 2026. 6 minute read.

If your toilet runs after flushing, hisses, or refills on its own every few minutes, the problem is almost always in the tank. The fill valve controls the water coming in, and the flapper controls the water going out. One of them is not sealing.

You can usually tell which in about five minutes with a few drops of food coloring. This guide shows how to test, adjust, and replace tank parts, and when a toilet repair visit makes sense instead.

Westminster note

City water in Westminster averages 252 mg/L of hardness, roughly 14.7 grains per gallon. Minerals build up on the fill valve seat and flapper edge, which keeps them from sealing tightly. If your toilet parts seem to fail every few years, wipe the flapper seat during any repair and expect to replace rubber parts sooner.

How the tank works in plain terms

.

When you flush, the handle lifts a chain that raises the flapper. Water drops into the bowl. The flapper falls back, the fill valve opens, and the tank refills until a float shuts the valve off. A tube called the overflow pipe catches any extra water before it spills onto the floor.

Each of these steps can fail on its own. Knowing which part does which job makes the diagnosis easy.

Fill valve or flapper: a two-minute dye test

.

Put 8 to 10 drops of food coloring in the tank. Do not flush. Wait 15 minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. If the tank water stays clear but you hear water running, look at the fill valve and the water level.

A fill valve that does not shut off sends water down the overflow pipe. Check the water line against the mark on the overflow tube. Water spilling into the tube means the float is set too high or the valve is worn.

Adjusting the float and chain before replacing anything

.

Sometimes the part is fine and only needs a tweak. Many fill valves have an adjustment screw or a clip on the rod that sets the water level. Lower it so the water sits about one inch below the top of the overflow pipe.

The chain should have a little slack, about half an inch, when the flapper is closed. Too tight and the flapper cannot seat. Too loose and it may not lift fully. Hook the chain at a different link to correct it.

Small change, big savings.

A toilet that leaks even a thin stream into the bowl can waste hundreds of gallons a month. A quiet leak is worth fixing quickly because you pay for every gallon.

Replacing a fill valve without making a mess

.

A fill valve swap takes about 30 minutes. Most universal valves fit most toilets. Check that the height adjusts to your tank, and note whether your tank has a side or bottom supply.

Drain the tank, then use a sponge to remove the last water. Loosen the supply nut under the tank with a wrench, then the large lock nut holding the valve. Lift out the old valve, drop in the new one, hand-tighten the nuts, and reconnect the water.

When the shutoff valve or supply line is the problem

.

Sometimes the leak is outside the tank. A dripping oval valve, a seeping braided line, or a loose nut can leave water on the floor. Old shutoff valves often seize or drip when turned, so if yours will not close fully, do not force it.

Replacing a supply line is quick if the valve works. If the valve itself is stuck, shut off the home's main water valve and call a plumber. A related fixture and valve repair can include replacing worn angle stops.

Signs the issue is bigger than the tank

.

Call a plumber if water leaks around the base of the toilet, if the bowl rocks, or if the toilet clogs repeatedly. Those point to the wax ring, the flange, or the drain line, not the tank valves.

If a toilet backs up while sinks or tubs gurgle, the blockage may be in the main line. That calls for sewer line cleaning, not new tank parts. Our phone line is answered 24/7 if water is spreading across the floor.

Toilet tank valve questions

.
Why does my toilet run on and off by itself?

A leaking flapper lets water slip into the bowl, which makes the fill valve kick on. Replace the flapper and clean the seat. This is called ghost flushing.

How long do fill valves and flappers last?

It varies, but rubber flappers often wear out in a few years. Plastic fill valves can last longer but fail with scale or debris. Replace them when they stop sealing.

Does hard water in Westminster damage toilet parts?

Water around 14.7 grains per gallon leaves mineral deposits on valve seats and flapper edges. This can cause slow leaks. Clean the seat and expect to replace rubber parts more often.

Can I fix a toilet that will not stop running myself?

Often yes. Start with the flapper and chain, then adjust or replace the fill valve. If the shutoff valve will not close, stop and call a plumber.

Why is my toilet tank not filling?

Check that the shutoff valve is fully open and the fill valve is not clogged with debris. A kinked supply line can also cut flow.

Yes. The phone line is answered 24/7. If the toilet is overflowing, close the oval valve behind it first, then call.
