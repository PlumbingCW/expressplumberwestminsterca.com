What Is a Plumbing Vent? A Westminster, CA Homeowner Guide

.

A plumbing vent is a pipe that lets air into your drain system so wastewater flows freely and sewer gas leaves the house.

Written by Westminster Express Plumbing. Published October 24, 2026. 6 minute read.

A plumbing vent is a pipe that runs from your drain lines up through the roof. It does two jobs. It lets fresh air into the system so water can drain without gurgling, and it carries sewer gas up and out above the roofline instead of into the rooms.

When a vent is blocked or missing, you notice it. Drains slow down, fixtures gurgle, toilets bubble when another fixture runs, and a sewer smell may drift into the bathroom. These signs are easy to confuse with a clogged drain, so knowing the difference saves money.

Below is how vents work, what goes wrong with them, and what to do. For a clog that is actually in the pipe, see drain cleaning.

Westminster note

Midway City Sanitary District serves all of Westminster and Midway City, at (714) 893-3553. If drains back up in several fixtures and venting is not the cause, ask the district or a plumber to confirm where property line responsibility starts before you decide who handles the repair.

How a vent keeps your drains moving

.

Think of pouring liquid from a bottle with one small hole. It glugs and slows. Drain pipes behave the same way. Water moving down a pipe pushes air ahead of it and pulls a vacuum behind it unless fresh air can enter.

The vent supplies that air. It also protects the water seal in each P-trap, the curved pipe under a sink. Without a vent, draining water can siphon the trap dry, and sewer gas then rises into the room.

Where the vents are in a typical house

.

Most homes have one or more vent stacks that rise through a wall and poke out of the roof, often near the back of the house. Some fixtures connect to the main stack, while others branch into their own vent lines inside the wall.

Walk outside and look at the roof for round pipes about two to four inches wide. Those are the vent terminations. In Westminster, where many homes have a single story, they are often visible from the yard.

Warning signs of a blocked or damaged vent

.

A single slow drain usually means a local clog. A pattern is different. If several fixtures gurgle, drain slowly or smell, a vent problem becomes more likely.

Birds' nests, leaves, tennis balls and even roof debris can block the opening. Inside the wall, an old pipe can crack, sag or fill with scale. Roof work and remodels can also cut a vent line by accident.

Vent problem or clog: how to tell

.

A clog usually affects one fixture and does not improve when other fixtures run. A vent issue often changes with what else is draining. Run a faucet and flush a toilet at the same time and see which fixtures complain.

If every drain backs up, or a floor drain bubbles, the problem may be the main line instead. In that case a sewer line cleaning visit is the place to start. A camera look can separate a clog from a collapsed pipe.

A quick test.

Pour a gallon of water slowly into a slow drain. If it drains quietly, the pipe was probably just dirty. If it gurgles loudly or backs up, the problem may be farther down, including the vent.

Air admittance valves: the vent without the roof pipe

.

An air admittance valve, often called an AAV or Studor-style valve, is a one-way mechanical vent. It opens to let air in when water drains, then closes to keep gas from escaping. It is often used under sinks or in remodels where running a pipe to the roof is hard.

AAVs wear out and can stick. A failing one lets air in but not out, or fails to open at all. Local rules govern where they are allowed, so ask the City Building Division at (714) 548-3254 before adding one. A traditional roof vent is usually the more dependable choice.

What 1970s Westminster homes can hide in the walls

.

The median Westminster house was built around 1971, and most neighborhoods grew in the 1960s. That means original drain and vent piping, often cast iron, galvanized steel or early plastic, is now many decades old. Old pipe corrodes from the inside, narrows and sometimes cracks.

The practical result is that a vent problem in an older house can come from a rusted section rather than a bird's nest. If cleaning the top does not help, a plumber may need to check the pipe in the wall. Serious corrosion may call for repiping a section of the system.

What you can safely check yourself

.

From the ground, look at the roof with binoculars for obvious blockages, bird nests or a missing cap. Do not climb on a roof to clear a vent unless you are experienced and have the right fall protection. Roof falls cause serious injuries every year.

Inside, check that every P-trap holds water and that nothing has been stored against a wall where a vent runs. If a seldom-used drain smells, run water for thirty seconds to refill the trap before you assume a bigger problem.

How a plumber diagnoses and fixes a vent

.

A plumber starts by listening to the symptoms and testing fixtures. A small camera or a measured flow of water down the vent can find the blockage. Clearing may involve a cable from the roof or a short run of new pipe if a section is damaged.

Standards and local sources.

Drain and vent layouts are set by the California Plumbing Code (Title 24, Part 5), which is why vent changes are a job for a licensed plumber. Verify a license on the Contractors State License Board website.

Plumbing vent questions from Westminster homeowners

.
Why do my drains gurgle?

Gurgling means air is being pulled through the water in a trap because the vent is not supplying enough. A blocked vent or a clog farther down the line is the usual cause.

Can a blocked vent cause a sewer smell?

Yes. A blocked vent can let a trap siphon dry, so sewer gas rises into the room. Refill the trap with water first, then look further.

Do all homes in Westminster have roof vents?

Almost every home with indoor plumbing has some form of venting. Older houses built around the median 1971 build year may have original piping, so a check is worthwhile if symptoms appear.

What is an air admittance valve?

It is a one-way valve that lets air into a drain line and keeps gas out. It can replace a roof vent in some spots, but it can fail and local rules apply.

Can I clear a vent myself?

You can look from the ground. Climbing on a roof is risky, so call a plumber if you suspect a blocked vent.

Is a vent problem urgent?

A gurgling drain can wait for a visit. A strong sewer smell or backing-up fixtures should be treated sooner, and our phone line is answered 24/7.
