How to Replace a Water Heater Thermostat in Westminster, CA

.

An electric water heater with lukewarm, scalding or no hot water often needs a new thermostat, and the job is doable if you cut power first.

Written by Westminster Express Plumbing. Published October 24, 2026. 6 minute read.

Electric tank heaters have two thermostats, an upper and a lower, plus a high-limit reset button. When one fails, you may get water that is never hot enough, water that runs out fast, or a tripped breaker. Replacing a thermostat is a modest repair, and the part is inexpensive compared with a new tank.

Safety comes first. The heater runs on 240 volts, and a mistake can seriously hurt you. This guide covers how to shut off power, test the parts, swap a thermostat, and decide when the tank is the real problem and water heater replacement makes more sense.

Westminster note

Westminster water averages 252 mg/L of total hardness, roughly 14.7 grains per gallon, and it ranges up to 413. That hardness leaves scale on heating elements, which makes them work harder and can mimic a thermostat problem. Before you buy a thermostat, flush the tank and check the elements, because scale is often the real reason the water runs out fast.

Is it really the thermostat? Symptoms to match first

.

Several different failures look alike from the shower. Matching your symptom to the likely part saves you from buying the wrong one. A heater with no hot water at all points to power, the high-limit switch, or the upper thermostat. Lukewarm water that runs out quickly often points to the lower thermostat or a bad lower element.

Turn the power off the right way

.

Switch off the double-pole breaker for the heater at the main panel. Tape over it or tell everyone in the house so no one turns it back on. Do not rely on the thermostat dial or a wall switch, since those do not kill the power to the wires.

Then confirm with a non-contact voltage tester or a multimeter at the thermostat terminals. If the tester still shows voltage, you have the wrong breaker or a wiring problem. Stop and do not touch the wires.

Never skip the voltage check.

A breaker label can be wrong, especially in older homes. A water heater has 240 volts across two live legs. Test the terminals yourself every time before your hand goes inside the access panel.

Tools and parts you need before you open the panel

.

Gather everything first so you are not hunting for parts with the cover off. Match the thermostat to your heater. Most are the snap-action type, but the mounting clips and the high-limit design vary, so bring the old part or the model label when you shop.

.

How to test the parts with a multimeter

.

With the power off and the wires removed, set the meter to ohms. A good element reads some resistance, often between 10 and 30 ohms depending on wattage. A reading of zero or infinity means the element has failed.

A thermostat can be checked for continuity across its terminals when the dial is set to call for heat. If it shows no continuity, replace it. If the thermostat tests fine and the element tests fine, the high-limit button or the wiring is next.

Scale, sediment and the hard water factor

.

Hot tanks in hard water areas collect scale on the elements and a layer of sediment on the bottom. A scaled lower element heats slowly and can burn out. A thick sediment layer insulates the water from the element, so the thermostat shuts off early.

Draining a few gallons from the bottom valve into a bucket tells you quickly if the tank is full of grit. If it comes out cloudy with chunks, flush the tank fully, and consider water softening or filtration to slow the buildup.

When to stop and call a plumber instead

.

Stop if the breaker keeps tripping, if you see burnt or melted wires, if water is leaking from the tank or fittings, or if you are not comfortable working near 240 volts. Those are signs of a bigger fault than a thermostat.

If the tank is older, a leak at the bottom or rusty water means the steel liner is failing and no thermostat will help. A water heater repair visit can sort out what is worth fixing. Our phone line is answered 24/7 for urgent failures.

Local rules, bracing and nearby towns

.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped. If you end up replacing the tank, the new one must be strapped for earthquake safety. Ask the City Building Division at (714) 548-3254 whether a permit applies to your project.

Water heater thermostat questions

.
Can I replace a water heater thermostat myself?

Many homeowners can, if they shut off the breaker and confirm no voltage. If you are unsure about working with 240 volts, call a plumber instead.

How do I know which thermostat failed, upper or lower?

No hot water at all usually points to the upper thermostat or high-limit. Lukewarm water that runs out quickly points to the lower. Testing with a multimeter confirms it.

What temperature should I set my water heater?

About 120 degrees F is a common setting for homes. It reduces scald risk and slows scale. Raise it only if you have a specific need.

Is hard water in Westminster a problem for my water heater?

Yes. Water averaging roughly 14.7 grains per gallon leaves scale on elements and sediment in the tank. Flushing the tank and checking elements helps hot water last longer.

Why does my water heater breaker keep tripping?

A shorted element or damaged wiring usually causes it. Do not keep resetting the breaker. Turn it off and call a plumber.

When is it time to replace the whole water heater?

Leaks from the tank, rusty hot water, or repeated failures mean the tank is wearing out. A new thermostat will not fix a corroded tank.
